# Interview Notes — LAB-009

**Source:** Retrospective Reconstruction

## Purpose

Prepare concise interview talking points from the presence and occupancy confidence-model work.

## 30-Second Summary

I designed a Home Assistant presence model that separated raw evidence from the final presence decision instead of treating one sensor as absolute truth. Testing showed that phone-side Wi-Fi and location information could become stale or unavailable in the local-only architecture, so I treated SSID association as corroborating evidence rather than an authoritative presence signal and deferred a stronger implementation until better network-side evidence becomes available.

## Problem / Business Need

Presence-based automations need to distinguish whether someone is actually home without creating unsafe or annoying behavior from unreliable sensor data.

A single presence signal can be stale, unavailable, or misleading.

## Technical Approach

The design separated:

- raw evidence sources
- evidence interpretation
- final presence state

Multiple signals could contribute to confidence rather than one signal automatically determining the result.

The model also distinguished missing evidence from contradictory evidence.

## Key Decision

Phone-side SSID information was retained as useful positive corroboration but was not trusted as the authoritative network-presence source.

## Troubleshooting / Challenge

Testing showed that some phone-derived presence information could become stale or unavailable because the Home Assistant environment was intentionally local-only.

That meant an apparently useful signal did not have the reliability required to become the sole basis for presence-sensitive automation.

## Security / Risk Considerations

False-positive presence can incorrectly conclude that someone is home.

False-negative presence can incorrectly conclude that the house is empty.

Those errors become increasingly important when presence controls security, locks, cameras, HVAC, or other consequential automation.

## Enterprise Relevance

This demonstrates:

- multi-signal decision logic
- confidence modeling
- evidence quality
- stale-data awareness
- false-positive/false-negative analysis
- graceful degradation
- requirements-driven architecture

## Interview Questions

### Why did you choose this approach?

Real-world sensor data is imperfect. I wanted the automation to reason from evidence rather than blindly trust one device state.

### What would you do differently in a production enterprise environment?

I would define signal authority, freshness thresholds, confidence scoring, observability, failure states, test cases, and documented behavior for unavailable or conflicting evidence.

### What did you learn from the project?

Raw signals are evidence, not truth. Missing evidence is also different from contradictory evidence, and freshness can matter as much as the value itself.

### What was the biggest technical or operational risk?

Building consequential automation on stale or unreliable presence information.
